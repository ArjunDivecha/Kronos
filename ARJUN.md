# ARJUN.md — product memo (Kronos fork)

*Fable 5, 2026-07-06. Written to you, not to an agent.*

## What this repo is worth

**Alive, and it's the one Kronos repo that actually trades.** The parent `Kronos` wrapper is just a
launcher; `Kronos Web` is the demo split. This fork is where the model, the backtests, and the live
Industry28 trader live — `pl_tracker.xlsx` and `trade_log.csv` show real fills since 2026-04-15. Keep it.
The research phase (the UK/Japan/India/SmallCap600 sweeps from April) is *done* — those were exploration to
find that Industry28 40/5 was the survivor. You don't need to keep re-running them; you need to keep the one
that won honest and running. The soft spot isn't the trading code (that's careful) — it's that the numbers
that justify trading were generated by scripts that silently drop tickers and reuse stale caches. Fix that
before you scale size, not after.

## Extensions ranked by value ÷ effort

1. **Auto-mark P/L to Bloomberg and reconcile against IBKR fills (highest leverage).** `pl_tracker.xlsx`
   currently marks positions at yfinance/last-close and was seeded by hand with your IBKR entry prices. Wire
   it to **OpusBloomberg** (`bbg.ref` for live marks, `bbg.hist` for cycle closes) and to the IBKR API you
   already use in the trader to pull *actual* fills, so cumulative P/L is broker-truth, not a hand-maintained
   spreadsheet. Why now: you're live with real money and a manual ledger is where the errors hide. First step:
   a `reconcile.py` that reads `trade_log.csv` + IBKR executions and rewrites the Positions tab. Reuses:
   OpusBloomberg, the existing `ib_insync` connection in `industry28_trader.py`.

2. **Nightly signal + P/L email, no laptop babysitting.** You run the trader by hand. Add a scheduled job
   (Railway cron or a `launchd`/`schedule` routine) that generates the Industry28 signals and current-cycle
   P/L each morning and emails/Slacks you the 3L/3S list and drift-from-target — *without* placing orders.
   Keep the human `YES` for actual trades. Why now: it turns "did I remember to check Kronos?" into a push.
   First step: reuse `generate_signals()` from the trader in a read-only script; deploy on Railway.

3. **Validate Industry28 on the QuantConnect 34-country-ETF harness.** You have a LEAN backtest skill with a
   clean survivorship-aware universe and equal-weight benchmark. Port the 40/5 top-3/bottom-3 rule there to get
   an *independent* Sharpe with realistic costs/slippage, out of your own yfinance pipeline. Why now: it's the
   cheapest way to sanity-check whether the +38.7%/2.14-Sharpe number survives contact with a second engine
   before you add size. First step: `/backtest` the Industry28 rule; compare to `industry28_metrics.txt`.

4. **Promote the temporal-leakage guard into a reusable check across the quant repos.** The guard you added to
   `KronosPredictor.predict` is genuinely good. The same as-of discipline is what the **T2 Factor Timing** and
   **Fable Daily Trading** repos need. Lift it into a tiny shared `asof_guard` util (or a personal-knowledge
   note) so every forecasting repo rejects look-ahead at the boundary. First step: extract the check into one
   function; import it in T2.

5. **Regime/vol overlay on position sizing.** Today sizing is flat: `net_liq * 0.98 / 6`. A simple vol-target
   (scale gross by trailing realized vol) is a well-worn Sharpe improver and you already have the price history
   cached. Lower priority than getting the ledger honest. First step: compute 20-day realized vol per cycle in
   the trader, scale `position_size`.

## Quick wins (< 1 hour each)

- **Delete stale `forecasts_*.csv` after any parameter change** — or you're backtesting last week's config
  (see FABLE.md P1). Until the fingerprint fix lands, this is a manual discipline; make it a habit.
- **Pin the model revision everywhere.** `etf_sweep.py` quietly uses `Kronos-small` while everything else uses
  `Kronos-base`; only the tests pin a HF revision. One-line grep + a shared constant kills a whole class of
  "why don't these two numbers agree" confusion (FABLE.md P2).
- **Commit or stash the 79 dirty files.** The working tree has modified scripts and untracked data dirs mixed
  in; you can't cleanly diff new work against it right now.

## What NOT to do

**Don't keep expanding the universe zoo.** You have UK500, Japan, India, SmallCap600, Universe500, TechStocks,
Factor, FixedIncome, Commodity, Sector — ~153 MB of committed forecast CSVs from the April exploration. That
was the right way to *find* the strategy, but building more universes now is motion, not progress: each one
adds survivorship-bias surface and more silent-failure exposure without telling you anything new about the one
strategy you actually trade. The marginal dollar of your time goes to making Industry28's evidence and P/L
bulletproof (items 1-3), not to a 12th backtest universe. Archive the exploration CSVs out of git and move on.
